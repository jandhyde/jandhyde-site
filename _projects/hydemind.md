**The problem.** There is no free, structured way to learn Revenue Operations as a whole. Cohort programs cost a few thousand dollars a year. Vendor certifications teach one tool, not the discipline. Social-media content is scattered and uneven. An MBA is expensive and not RevOps-specific.

**The goal.** A free, structured RevOps curriculum modeled on the open-source computer-science degrees that organize public resources into a real path. Order the best available material into a progression, write in an honest practitioner voice, and keep it free with no commercial offering attached.

**What I did.** I structured the content as six pillars -- data, customer processes, KPIs and reporting, tech stack, enablement, and strategic insights -- with cross-cutting pages on using LLMs for RevOps and on marketing operations, plus ops-discipline lens pages for sales ops and CS ops that reference the pillar content rather than duplicate it. I set an anonymization standard so real practitioner examples could be used without fingerprints: strip company names, exact figures, product names, geography, and timeframes, but keep the role, the universal pattern, relative numbers, outcomes, and lessons. I curated instead of authoring from scratch, organizing roughly 150 vetted external links into the progression behind a homepage built around core KPIs and a pillar grid. And I built it as a static site so it stays cheap and maintainable for one person.

**The output.** A live, free site at hydemind.com, launched January 2026: about a dozen content pages -- the six pillars, the cross-cutting and ops-discipline pages, and a learning-path page -- backed by a curated library of around 150 links. No paywall, no sign-up.

**Wider application.** It is public and free for anyone learning RevOps. It runs no analytics by design, so reach is deliberately unmeasured and no adoption is claimed. The reusable part is the method -- curate the best free material, anonymize real examples to a fixed standard, and order it into a weighted progression rather than a link dump.
