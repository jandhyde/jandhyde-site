## What it's about

Forte's argument is that information you encounter is too valuable to lose, and the standard moves -- bookmarking, scrolling past, "I'll come back to this" -- guarantee that you do. He proposes a method called CODE (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express) and a structure called PARA (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) for sorting whatever you capture into something you can actually find later.

## What stuck

PARA is the load-bearing piece. The rest of the book reinforces it but doesn't depend on it.

The classification PARA forces is what does the work. A Project has an outcome and a finish line. An Area has standards to maintain but never completes -- you don't finish health, finances, or your job. A Resource is a topic you might care about later. Treating these as the same thing is how knowledge systems quietly turn into junk drawers.

The other thing that stuck was Forte's framing of notes as packets sent to your future self. The act of distilling something today -- a meeting, a conversation, a half-formed idea -- so a later version of you can act on it without rebuilding context is the actual point of capture. Files you can't act on later are storage, not knowledge.

## Where I use it

I used PARA explicitly to structure a five-year personal plan in early 2025 -- sorting things like a degree finish, a debt payoff, and a weight target as Projects (they have outcomes), and things like Career Development, Financial Health, and Knowledge Management as Areas (they don't). Forcing each item into one bucket made it visible where I'd been treating ongoing maintenance like a project I could finish.

A year later I ran the same lens over my Notion workspace and it surfaced concrete schema drift -- a Projects database with an Area/Resource Type field, an Areas/Resources database with a Done status (Areas don't complete by definition), and a couple of pages that were quietly functioning as dumping grounds. PARA's value as a diagnostic for an existing system, not just a structure for a new one, is the part the book undersells.
