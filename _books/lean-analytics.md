## What it's about

Lean Analytics is about figuring out what to measure and when. The authors build a framework around the "One Metric That Matters" -- the single number a business should obsess over at any given stage of its life -- and walk through stage-specific versions of it for SaaS, marketplaces, e-commerce, content, and a few others.

## What stuck

The argument that most teams don't have a metrics problem -- they have a focus problem. Dashboards full of metrics generally mean nobody has decided which one actually drives the business right now. The book pushes hard on the idea that adding a metric is cheap and adding a decision rule tied to that metric is expensive, and only the second one matters.

The other thing that stuck: the metrics hierarchy is supposed to change as the business changes. The metric that mattered at the empathy stage isn't the metric that matters at the scale stage. Holding onto an old top-line metric is one of the quieter ways teams stall.

## Where I use it

Mostly when somebody hands me a request that's really an unstated decision. "Can you build a dashboard that shows X?" usually means "I want to feel better about X" or "I'm going to argue for Y in a meeting and I need a chart." Lean Analytics gave me a habit of asking what decision the data is supposed to support before I touch it. If there's no decision behind the request, the dashboard is theater.

I also use the One Metric That Matters framing when scoping work for a team. If we can't agree on the one metric for the next quarter, we're going to drift. If we can, the rest of the prioritization work gets a lot easier.
